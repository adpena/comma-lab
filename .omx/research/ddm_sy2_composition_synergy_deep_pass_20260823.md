## W96 derivation

The exact W96 single-FiLM archive is **179,290 B** with `d_seg=0.00029384`,
`d_pose=0.0137999`, and recomputed **S=0.5202480181682149 `[contest-CUDA T4, n600]`**. The pose
term is the failure; the 1,078 B rate saving is real but tiny.

Freeze the counterfactual required by the charter: the exact-object compensation lands pose at or
below DX2's base and the measured W96 d_seg holds. MAIN's full-precision break-even then says the
archive must be **<=166,484 B**, or **12,806 B** smaller than W96.

If the carrier is the only mutable byte pool:

`22,010 B - 12,806 B = 9,204 B`.

So W96 is arithmetically reachable only if all of the following become true on the same final object:

1. the W96 renderer is trained/compiled in its intended form rather than treated as a frozen weight
   projection;
2. a fresh carrier solve against W96 lands `d_pose <= 6.37e-6` after parse-back, not merely on a
   cached render;
3. the carrier's complete coded section is at most 9,204 B if no other section saves bytes;
4. d_seg does not exceed the measured W96 component used by the bound;
5. the canonical archive and independent repeat match and all paid sections are consumed.

QS5 proves the compensation instrument on its exact object, not the W96 constant. JG5 independently
shows that a same-object carrier re-solve can put many edited renders at or below base pose. FS3 is
the counter-warning: a stale-carrier edit required 696x pose recovery where the measured re-solve
delivered about 8x. These receipts jointly make “same object” the causal condition; neither proves
W96 reaches 9,204 B.

Even success only returns W96 to the DX2 frontier. At its displayed d_seg and base pose, sub-0.12
needs an archive around **124,102 B**, 55,188 B below current W96. After paying the 12,806 B tie
requirement, roughly the full **42,382 B** campaign gap remains. W96 therefore becomes strategically
useful only if it creates a better basis for the semantic/model representation; carrier shrink alone
cannot achieve the target.
