## 0. The problem, restated in carrier terms (so the survey is rankable)

The contest score (VERIFIED from the repo README, https://github.com/commaai/comma_video_compression_challenge):

```
score = 100 * d_seg  +  sqrt(10 * d_pose)  +  25 * (archive_bytes / original_bytes)
```

- `d_seg` = mean per-pixel **argmax disagreement** of a frozen SegNet (5-class) on the **last frame** of each pair. (boundary-only signal; flat gradient except at decision boundaries)
- `d_pose` = MSE of a frozen PoseNet (FastViT-T12, YUV6 12-channel) on the **two-frame** ego-motion (6 dims).
- `rate` = compressed archive / original (original ≈ 37.5 MB). Current frontier rate ≈ 0.118 at ~162-178 KB.

The carrier question (operator's): **what per-pair representation gives the best rate-distortion** for (a) a frame whose SegNet-argmax = GT partition, (b) a frame0↔frame1 differential yielding GT PoseNet ego-motion, at minimum archive bytes — beating the HNeRV-decoder + per-pair-28-d-latent (VQ-crushed-to-8-bits) capstone, target sub-0.15.

**Public frontier (VERIFIED, GitHub PR list):**
- **PR #112 `rhnerv_comma` = 0.191** (mattneel) — current best; byte-identical decode to PR #110, pure entropy-recoding win (see §3.A).
- **PR #110 `hnerv_fec6_fixed_huffman_k16` = 0.192** (adpena — our lane).
- PR #101 `hnerv_ft_microcodec` = 0.193 (GOLD), PR #102/#103/#100 = 0.195. PRs #113-115 carry no new carrier technique (checked; none post-#112).
All public leaders are **HNeRV-decoder + per-frame/per-pair latent + entropy bolt-ons.** No public submission has yet swapped the carrier itself. This is the opening.

Two carrier sub-problems, with different SOTA answers:
1. **The content carrier** (paints a sharp frame → low d_seg). Spectral-bias is the enemy: a residual-free NeRV blurs to a mean-field and SegNet collapses to one class (sister memo §2, d_seg=0.5075 `[macOS-MLX research-signal]`).
2. **The motion/temporal carrier** (frame0↔frame1 delta → low d_pose). The "pose-impoverishment bug": crushing the per-pair latent to 8 bits destroys the inter-frame structure PoseNet reads.

## 1. RANKED carrier recommendations (lead with the 2-3 to engineer)

Ranked by likely fit to OUR geometry (frozen-scorer-only, single-video overfit, sub-0.15 byte budget). Each: carrier → rate-distortion (sourced) → score-geometry fit → adopt/adapt/reject.

### RANK 1 — ADAPT: Wavelet store-LF / generate-HF carrier (SNeRV / FANeRV / HFNeRV family) — the spectral-bias-native content carrier

- **Carrier.** Per-frame: DWT split into low-frequency (LF) approximation + high-frequency (HF) detail. **Store only the LF as the carrier; GENERATE the HF with a decoder block** (SNeRV's MFU/HFR; FANeRV's Wavelet Frequency Upgrade Block; HFNeRV's WFD+HFM). SNeRV adds a **TUB (temporally-extended up-sampling block)** that models the frame0↔frame1 temporal correlation — directly the pose carrier.
- **Rate-distortion (sourced).**
  - SNeRV (ECCV 2024): "encodes only the LF... HF generated by decoder"; compression = 10% prune + **8-bit decoder / 6-bit embeddings**, model → 1/2-1/3 size; "outperforms existing NeRV models in capturing fine details." (https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01681, https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/07231.pdf)
  - FANeRV (May 2025): Wavelet Frequency Upgrade Block (DWT split + targeted HF enhancement) "significantly improves reconstruction... outperforming existing NeRV methods" on compression/inpainting/interpolation. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06755)
  - FaNeRV (Jan 2026): progressive frequency-decoupled learning + dynamic HF injection; "**surpasses the state-of-the-art hybrid codec VTM**" in RD. (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17741)
  - HFNeRV (2411.06685): wavelet HF encoder + HF feature modulation to fix the "reconstructed videos lack high-frequency details" problem. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06685)
- **Why it fits our geometry.** d_seg lives on **argmax boundaries = high-frequency edges**. The entire NeRV-family failure we observe (sister memo H1/H4: mean-field → one-class collapse) is *spectral bias* — and this family is the literature's direct architectural answer to spectral bias. HFR/HFM explicitly restore the edges that the LF path blurs. The TUB restores the two-frame pose signal. "Store-LF / generate-HF" is also the **best byte story** (LF is a small fraction of DWT coefficients → aligns with `25*bytes`).
- **Caveat (from sister memo §4, INFERRED).** HF is *generated* not *stored*, so boundary fidelity depends on the LF→HF predictor generalizing on THIS video; under aggressive byte pressure the HF generator can collapse to ~0 (back to LF-only blur — the same disease, different door). For single-video overfit, a *stored* residual (Rank-2 PR95-skip) may be more reliable. And SNeRV's official MFU/HFR/TUB **export/runtime binding is unproven** (AGENTS.md SNeRV hard blocker).
- **Verdict: ADAPT.** Strongest *principled* content carrier and the one with a real pose-temporal story (TUB). Engineer it as a parallel campaign behind Rank-2. Port target: **SNeRV OSS `github.com/qwertja/SNeRV`** (DWT + MFU + HFR + TUB reference); FANeRV's Wavelet Frequency Upgrade Block as the HF module. First experiment is a non-byte-closed recon smoke: does HFR actually recover edges (PSNR>30, d_seg<0.1) or collapse to LF?

### RANK 2 — ADOPT NOW: PR95 bilinear-skip + refine-residual on the existing HNeRV carrier (the one-line spectral-bias fix)

- **Carrier.** Keep the existing HNeRV decoder + per-pair latent, but make each upsample block residual: `sin(PixelShuffle(conv(x)) + bilinear_up(x))` + terminal `x + 0.1*sin(refine(x))`. This is HiNeRV's documented mechanism (bilinear-up + hierarchical encoding) and PR95's reference decoder.
- **Rate-distortion (sourced).** HiNeRV's own ablation: removing hierarchical encoding drops PSNR **34.69 → 32.16 dB** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09818, https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/e5dc475c370ff42f2f96dddf8191a40c-Paper-Conference.pdf). HiNeRV overall: **72.3% BD-rate saving over HNeRV, 43.4% over DCVC** on UVG (PSNR). This is the single biggest RD lever in the NeRV family and it's a residual-connectivity + grid-PE change, not a new carrier.
- **Why it fits.** It is the **cheapest** path (sister memo F1: ~10-20 lines), is already byte-closed/exported on our lane, and directly attacks the mean-field root cause that collapses both d_seg and d_pose. It does NOT change the carrier *bytes* (same latent), so the rate term is unchanged — it improves distortion-at-fixed-rate, which is exactly the axis to move from 0.19→sub-0.15.
- **Verdict: ADOPT (immediate).** This is the dominant near-term move per the sister memo's F1; the external SOTA (HiNeRV's 72.3% over HNeRV) is the quantitative justification. It is NOT a new carrier — it makes the existing carrier actually work — but it is the prerequisite that unblocks every carrier comparison (you can't rank carriers against a broken baseline).

### RANK 3 — ADOPT (carrier-byte fix): FSQ (Finite Scalar Quantization) to replace the 8-bit VQ crush — fixes the pose-impoverishment bug directly

- **Carrier.** Replace the per-pair-latent VQ (codebook nearest-neighbor → 8-bit index) with **FSQ**: project the latent to a few dims (<10), quantize each dim to a small fixed grid; the implicit codebook = product of per-dim value sets.
- **Rate-distortion (sourced).** FSQ (https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15505): "**does not suffer from codebook collapse**, does not need commitment losses / reseeding / code splitting / entropy penalties"; "competitive performance" vs VQ across generation + dense prediction at matched codebook size. Corroborated by the FSQ-audio result: "FSQ uses a simple fixed-grid... results in **almost complete codebook utilization**, requires no auxiliary losses" (https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09550v1). General VQ-vs-scalar finding: VQ "exploits inter-feature dependencies" but **codebook collapse — unused codewords — remains a central issue at low bitrate** (https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/vector-quantization-vq-vae).
- **Why it fits.** The operator's "VQ-crushed to 8 bits = pose impoverishment bug" is exactly **codebook collapse + information bottleneck**: a small VQ codebook discards the smooth inter-frame structure PoseNet reads, and the surviving codes don't span the pose manifold. FSQ's full-utilization fixed grid preserves more of the latent's continuous structure at the same bit budget — and the per-pair latent is **memoryless with negative autocorrelation ~−0.45** (VERIFIED from PR #112's own analysis, https://github.com/commaai/comma_video_compression_challenge/pull/112), which is precisely the regime where a per-dim scalar grid (FSQ) + simple AR residual coding is near-optimal and a learned vector codebook is wasteful.
- **Verdict: ADOPT (carrier-byte layer).** Drop-in replacement for the VQ bottleneck; preserves pose-relevant latent structure at equal bytes. Port target: FSQ reference `github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/fsq` (also lucidrains `vector-quantize-pytorch` has FSQ). Pair with Rank-2 (sharp render) so the latent actually *carries* pose structure to preserve.

### RANK 4 — ADAPT (motion carrier): explicit pose + frozen warp (FFNeRV-style flow), store 6-dim pose at ~0 extra bytes

- **Carrier.** Store the **6-dim ego-motion pose per pair explicitly** (it's tiny — 6 floats/pair × 600 pairs ≈ 14 KB raw, far less entropy-coded since dashcam ego-motion is smooth/low-entropy) and **warp frame0→frame1** with a flow derived from pose + a frozen depth/geometry prior, FFNeRV-style. FFNeRV: "warps nearby independent frames with flow maps and combines them... weighted sum of warped frames using predicted flow, softmax-normalized" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.12294, https://maincold2.github.io/ffnerv/). FFNeRV "outperforms H.264/HEVC, on par with SOTA" among frame-wise INRs.
- **Why it fits / why partial.** PoseNet is *literally* a pose estimator; if the render reproduces the true pose-relevant temporal structure, d_pose → 0. Storing pose explicitly is the most byte-efficient pose carrier *if* a frozen warp can turn pose into the right frame0↔frame1 delta. **Risk:** the warp must be accurate enough that the *warped* frame's PoseNet output matches GT — and a generic warp can't model occlusion/parallax in a dashcam scene; FFNeRV mitigates by *aggregating* warped + independent frames. For our single-video overfit, an *overfitted* flow grid (Cool-Chic-video style, Rank-5) may beat a pose-derived analytic warp.
- **Verdict: ADAPT (motion sub-carrier), behind Rank-2.** The "6-dim pose + frozen warp ≈ near-exact at ~0 extra bytes" hypothesis is *plausible but unproven* — the warp fidelity is the open question. Worth a cheap probe: store GT pose, warp, score d_pose. If a simple warp gets d_pose < 1e-3, this is a huge byte win on the pose carrier. Port target: FFNeRV flow-aggregation OSS `github.com/maincold2/FFNeRV`.

### RANK 5 — ADAPT (long shot, best byte story): Cool-Chic / C3 overfitted latent-grid carrier (tiny decoder, autoregressive Laplacian latent)

- **Carrier.** Overfitted per-frame **multi-resolution latent feature grids** + a tiny synthesis MLP (Cool-Chic: ~1000 multiplications/pixel; Cool-Chic-video: **800 parameters total**) + **autoregressive Laplacian entropy model** decoding each latent from its spatial context. (https://c3-neural-compression.github.io/, https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02156, https://github.com/Orange-OpenSource/Cool-Chic)
- **Rate-distortion (sourced).** Cool-Chic: visual quality similar to **H.266/VVC with 30% less rate**; C3: matches VTM (image) / Video Compression Transformer (video) at **<3k-5k MACs/pixel** decode — an order of magnitude lower than neural baselines at similar RD (https://c3-neural-compression.github.io/). Cool-Chic-video does motion with **800 parameters** (https://hal.science/hal-04596496 — Anubis-blocked at fetch time; numbers from the search abstract).
- **Why it fits / why long shot.** It is the **smallest-decoder** carrier in the literature (the `25*bytes` term loves a sub-KB decoder) and its overfit-per-video design is native to our single-video regime. The autoregressive Laplacian latent is exactly the entropy-optimal model for our smooth/memoryless per-pair latent. **BUT** (a) it is a generic RD codec — it optimizes PSNR/MS-SSIM, NOT d_seg/d_pose argmax geometry, so it will spend bytes on perceptually-salient regions the scorer ignores; (b) the autoregressive decode is **sequential per-latent**, a 30-min-inflate runtime risk on 1200 frames; (c) it has a known **FP4A/export-gate** history in our lab (CLAUDE.md: Cool-Chic / C3 are DEFERRED-pending-export-design). Adapt only if the score-aware loss can be grafted onto its synthesis MLP.
- **Verdict: ADAPT (research lane, not next).** Best byte story; wrong default objective; runtime + export risk. Revisit after Rank-2/3 land.

### RANK 6 — REJECT (as the primary carrier): generic end-to-end neural video codecs (DCVC-FM/DCVC-DC, Ballé hyperprior)

- **Carrier.** Per-frame latent + scale-hyperprior / temporal-conditional entropy model. DCVC-FM: **20.3% bitrate saving over VTM (RGB), 24.1% (YUV420)**, intra-period 32 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17414, https://github.com/microsoft/DCVC). Ballé 2018 scale hyperprior is the canonical `bits = -log2 p_y(y)` latent entropy model (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.01436).
- **Why it does NOT fit as primary.** These are *amortized* codecs (one model for all videos) optimized for PSNR/MS-SSIM across a distribution — the opposite of single-video overfit, and the wrong objective (not d_seg/d_pose). Their **decoder is large** (millions of params → terrible `25*bytes` for a single 37.5MB video). They are dominated by overfit-per-video INRs at our extreme single-video, tiny-archive operating point.
- **Verdict: REJECT as carrier; ADOPT the entropy-coding components.** The hyperprior / conditional-Gaussian-or-Laplacian latent entropy model and ANS/range-coding ARE the right tooling for the carrier-byte layer (this is what PR #112 already did — see §3.A). Mine these for the entropy model, not the architecture.

### RANK 7 — REJECT: coordinate networks (SIREN/FINER/WIRE) and instant-NGP hash grids as the *per-pair content* carrier

- **Carrier.** SIREN/FINER/WIRE = coordinate-MLP `f(x,y,t)→RGB`; instant-NGP = multi-resolution hash grid + tiny MLP.
- **Why it does NOT fit.** (a) Coordinate-MLPs paint *all* spatial structure through the MLP — no per-frame content latent — so they are *less* byte-efficient than HNeRV's content-adaptive embedding for video (the NeRV family beat pure-coordinate INRs precisely on this). (b) **SIREN's high-frequency `sin(w·x)` is HARD-EARNED for coordinate inputs but CARGO-CULTED on coordinate-free feature maps** — this is literally our current `w=30` spectral-bias trap (sister memo H4). (c) instant-NGP hash grids are a 3D-scene tool; CAwa-NeRF gets a single masked object to **0.53-1.2 MB** (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.14695) — orders of magnitude over our 162 KB budget for a full dashcam minute.
- **Useful sub-component (ADAPT):** HiNeRV's **hierarchical multi-resolution feature grid** IS an instant-NGP-style grid used *as positional encoding* fused into the NeRV decoder (sister memo F2, `use_hierarchical_feature_grid`). That is the right way to borrow grid encoding — as PE, not as the whole carrier. FINER's variable-periodic activation could replace the `w=30` sin to relieve spectral bias *if* coordinate input is reintroduced via the grid PE.
- **Verdict: REJECT as primary carrier; the grid-as-PE sub-component is Rank-2's F2.**

## 2. Anything that beats HNeRV + 28-d-latent on rate-at-fixed-distortion (the operator's flag)

Sorted by confidence that it beats the current carrier on **distortion-at-fixed-rate** (the axis that moves 0.19→sub-0.15):

1. **HiNeRV hierarchical encoding (Rank-2):** YES, with the strongest external number — **72.3% BD-rate over HNeRV** on UVG (https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09818). This is decisive and cheap; it is the bilinear-skip + grid-PE the sister memo found MISSING from our carrier.
2. **Wavelet store-LF/generate-HF (Rank-1, FaNeRV):** YES on RD — FaNeRV "surpasses VTM" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17741); SNeRV/FANeRV/HFNeRV all beat HNeRV on fine detail. Confidence high on *distortion*; medium on *our* d_seg specifically (boundary argmax ≠ PSNR).
3. **FSQ over VQ-8bit (Rank-3):** YES on the *pose carrier* specifically — full codebook utilization vs collapse at the same bits (https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15505). Fixes the impoverishment bug; doesn't change rate.
4. **Cool-Chic/C3 (Rank-5):** YES on *byte budget* (sub-KB decoder, VVC−30% rate) but on PSNR-RD, not d_seg/d_pose-RD; needs the score-aware graft.

Everything else (DCVC, Ballé, SIREN, NGP) does NOT beat the current carrier *at our operating point* (single-video, tiny-archive, frozen-scorer) as a primary carrier, though their entropy-coding pieces do help the byte layer.

## 3. The byte/entropy layer the public frontier is already exploiting (context, so carrier work doesn't re-derive it)

### 3.A — PR #112 `rhnerv` (0.191) is a pure entropy-recoding win on the SAME carrier (VERIFIED, PR #112)

This matters because it tells you **how much byte headroom is left in entropy coding alone vs how much must come from the carrier** (https://github.com/commaai/comma_video_compression_challenge/pull/112):
- Decoder weights: 7 Brotli streams → **per-tensor adaptive range coding (constriction ANS, geometric-primed priors)**, saving ~1,060 B.
- Latent blob: raw-LZMA1 → **causal-prediction (per-dim AR(1) + cross-dim predictors, integer-quantized LS coeffs, discrete-Gaussian residual coding)**, saving 317 B.
- Total: 178,517 → 177,136 B (−1,381 B), score 0.192→0.191. **Decode +0.2 s CPU.**
- KEY carrier-relevant finding: the latents are **memoryless with negative autocorrelation ~−0.45**; "Brotli q11 sits ~950 B above per-tensor order-0 adaptive cost." This is the empirical signature that (i) the per-pair latent has weak temporal structure to exploit (so a *better carrier* that injects more pose structure is where the big win is, not more entropy coding), and (ii) a **per-dim scalar grid + AR(1) residual (FSQ + AR, Rank-3) is near-optimal** for this latent — confirming the Rank-3 recommendation from the live frontier itself.

**Implication for carrier work:** entropy coding has squeezed the existing carrier to within ~1 KB of its order-0 floor. The remaining 0.04 to sub-0.15 must come from **distortion** (Rank-1/2: sharper render lowers d_seg/d_pose) and from a **carrier that needs fewer bytes for the same distortion** (Rank-1 store-LF, Rank-5 tiny-decoder), NOT from more entropy coding on the current latent.

### 3.B — Latent entropy models to reuse (Rank-6 components)
Conditional Laplacian/Gaussian latent priors + ANS range coding (Cool-Chic's autoregressive Laplacian; Ballé's scale hyperprior `bits=-log2 p_y(y)`; constriction ANS already in PR #112). These are the carrier-byte toolkit; the carrier produces the latent, these code it.

## 4. The 2-3 to engineer first (the lead recommendation)

1. **Rank-2 NOW (one-line, unblocks everything):** add PR95 bilinear-skip + refine-residual (+ turn on the hierarchical grid PE) to the existing HNeRV carrier. External justification: HiNeRV 72.3% BD-rate over HNeRV (https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09818). This is the sister memo's F1/F2; it is a distortion win at unchanged rate. **Without it, no carrier comparison is valid** (the baseline is a broken skip-free NeRV).
2. **Rank-3 in parallel (carrier-byte, fixes the pose bug):** swap the 8-bit VQ latent crush for FSQ (https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15505). The live frontier's own latent analysis (PR #112: memoryless, neg-autocorr) says a per-dim scalar grid is the right model. Cheap; preserves pose structure at equal bytes.
3. **Rank-1 as the parallel campaign (the real class-shift):** port SNeRV's DWT store-LF/generate-HF + TUB (`github.com/qwertja/SNeRV`) — the literature's direct answer to the spectral-bias root cause AND the only family with a native pose-temporal carrier (TUB). FaNeRV-class results surpass VTM. Higher effort (export/runtime binding is the SNeRV hard blocker), highest ceiling.

Then probe **Rank-4** (explicit pose + frozen warp) as a cheap byte-win experiment on the pose carrier, and hold **Rank-5** (Cool-Chic/C3) for a tiny-decoder byte campaign after Rank-2/3 land.
