bytes.** Rate and seg are the axes the corpus argues about; pose is the one that has never once been
inside the box. **Consequence, and I would carry this as the arm's most transferable line: a

**3. Ranked candidates, with the nearest refusal bracket named for each** (§4). The three that

## 1. The screen (DERIVED, and not original to me)

The score is `S = λ_B·B + 100·d_seg + √(10·d_pose)` with `λ_B = 6.658590e-07` S/B, CITED from
`ddm_tx1` §0. Since `B ≥ 0`:

> **ZERO-BYTE ADMISSION TEST.** An object with `distortion_only_S = 100·d_seg + √(10·d_pose) ≥ 0.12`
> can never reach sub-0.12, at any rate, by any coder, after any composition.
> Otherwise its largest admissible archive is `B_max = (0.12 − distortion_only_S) / λ_B`.

**Attribution, because this is a re-presentation, not a discovery.** `ddm_fb1` §4 already ran the
free-archive row on dx2's own axes (*"BOTH distortions → ZERO … +0.000100"*), and `ddm_wq1` §3 ran it
on two objects (dx2 `0.2343× of 0.12`; TR1 `2.489× its frontier`). What is new here is applying it as
a **screen across every measured alternative object** and publishing `B_max` per object. The
arithmetic is theirs; the table is the contribution.

**Calibration cross-check (it reproduces a verified constant from a different direction).**
`B_max(dx2) = (0.12 − 0.0281202)/λ_B = 137,987 B`, and the campaign's independently verified strict
floor is *"137,986 B passes (S 0.1199994415), 137,987 fails."* The equality point lands exactly one
byte above the last passing size, which is what a strict inequality requires. The screen is
calibrated, not asserted.

Two things follow that the corpus has not put side by side:

**The pose budget is not a fixed `1.25e-4`; it opens as bytes fall — and it still is not enough.**
The circulating figure (`Δd_pose ≤ 1.25e-4` to consume the whole gap) is correct **at the current
operating point**, where bytes are held. An object that also sheds 40% of the archive gets a `d_pose`
ceiling of `2.5e-4`, and at a free archive `9.97e-4` — **157× dx2's**. That is real relief and it
should be stated, because "pose may rise by 1.25e-4" reads as a law when it is a corner. **It changes
nothing about the verdict:** the best non-dx2-lineage `d_pose` ever measured is W96's `0.0137999`,
**13.84× over even the free-archive ceiling**, and every other alternative object is 10³–10⁶ over.

**So the ordering of construction is wrong everywhere it has been tried.** Every alternative object
in §2 was built field-first or renderer-first and had pose bolted on, re-solved, or left to the
`22,010 B` carrier fitted to a frame that then changed. `ddm_gd3` measured the consequence directly:
*"the pose carrier is a fitted artifact of the decoded frame_1; any token-side change invalidates
it"* — its probe moved `d_pose 0.00517 → 76.19`. JG5 measured that a **same-object** carrier re-solve
returns many edited renders to at-or-below base pose; FS3 measured a **stale** carrier missing the
required recovery by 696× where the re-solve delivered ~8×. The mechanism exists and is measured.
What has never happened is making it a **construction constraint** instead of a repair.

> **DESIGN RULE proposed for every future new-object charter (DERIVED, not measured).** Fix the pose
> mechanism first and gate on it: demonstrate a parse-back `d_pose ≤ 1e-4` **on the new object**
> before spending a slot on its field or renderer representation. An object that fails that gate has
> `B_max < 0` and its byte story is unpriceable — which is, in one line, why nine objects were built
> and none was ever admissible.

| 5,000 | **29.6%** |

111,276`), so the two arithmetics agree; the difference is that a **replacement** reclaims 13,515 B

**The bracket this candidate must beat, stated against itself.** Three concordant measurements say

## 5. THE EXECUTED FALSIFIER — regular coded-support reduction is CLOSED

**Why this direction and not another.** `ddm_tba1` §6 records the invariant no arm has broken:

> *"The invariant across every prior arm that produced a byte number: `N` was held at exactly
> 117,964,800. Not one changed how many positions are coded."*

tba1's D2a/D2b close *subset* reduction, because a chosen subset must be NAMED and the selector tax
exceeds the prize at every threshold (best net **+9.45 B**, +9.90 B under colex). **A regular lattice
is the one support reduction that pays no selector tax at all**: the kept set is a global,
receiver-derivable rule whose description length is O(1) and does not grow with `|kept|` — address-FREE
by `af1` §3's own mechanical criterion. So its entire price is *reconstruction distortion*, and that is
a property of the FIELD. **No scorer, no renderer, no training, and — decisively — no retrain can move
it: no amount of training lets a decoder recover information that was never transmitted.**

**Instrument.** `experiments/ddm_no1_lattice_reduction_floor.py`, `[macOS-CPU scorer-free advisory]`,
52.3 s, n600, full population (all 117,964,800 positions), on the sha-pinned decoded dx2 token field
`cc10a7b0…` (re-hashed this run, **matches the `ddm_tba1` charter pin**). Four rules per lattice:

- **nearest** — replicate the kept sample (1 coarse neighbour). ACHIEVED.
- **bilinear** — one-hot bilinear + argmax (4 coarse neighbours; what a smooth learned upsampler
  approximates). ACHIEVED.
- **oracle4 / oracle9** — the in-sample Bayes-optimal predictor on the 2×2 / 3×3 coarse neighbourhood.
  **LOWER BOUNDS** on any deterministic rule reading that neighbourhood, fitted and scored on the same
  data, so optimistic even within their own class.

**Two controls, both passed.** The `1×1` identity lattice returned **0** errors under all rules. The
orientation control reproduced `ddm_tba1` §2's per-class areas to **4.85e-05 pp** and its row centroids
