## Shared mathematical kernel

For Paper A, the importance of weight `W_ij` for task `q` is

```text
S^q_ij = sum_{k=1}^N |W_ij| ||X^q_{jk}||_2,
T_math = TopK(S^math) \ TopK(S^non-math).
```

The top sets are computed separately per layer over attention and MLP weights. The authors then
validate the selected set causally by either pruning it (`W_ij <- 0`) or multiplying it by a small
model-dependent constant.

Paper B retains the same operation at token resolution:

```text
S_l,ij(P) = sum_b sum_t |W_l,ij| ||x^(b,t)_l,j||_2,
M_l,ij = 1[(i,j) in C_l \ N_l],
W'_l = W_l odot (1 + alpha M_l).
```

`C_l` and `N_l` are the top-`rho` creative and non-creative importance sets. Paper B replaces
labeled task examples with six sets of contrastive prompts.

Neither expression is a gradient, a Fisher metric, an input-costate, a flip-distance, a causal
effect estimate, or an MDL/rate term. Its appeal is computational: it needs forward activations and
model weights only.

Pact's authority objective remains

```text
S = 100*d_seg + sqrt(10*d_pose) + 25*archive_bytes/37_545_489.
```

The paper metrics have no authority until a proposed mechanism survives the real receiver,
`R`/uint8, frozen scorers, exact counted bytes, and ultimately contest CPU/CUDA replay.

## Exact bounded check — set exclusion is not a safety certificate

The following deterministic NumPy construction mirrors the papers' `Top_target \ Top_protected`
selection and 10% multiplicative scaling. The protected top coordinate is 1, so coordinate 0 survives
the set difference. The protected objective couples both coordinates:

```python
import numpy as np
w = np.array([1.0, 1.0])
s_target = np.array([2.0, 1.0])
s_protect = np.array([1.0, 2.0])
mask = np.array([i in {np.argmax(s_target)} - {np.argmax(s_protect)} for i in range(2)])
w2 = w * (1.0 + 0.1 * mask)
protected = lambda z: float(np.sum(z) ** 2)
print(np.flatnonzero(mask), protected(w), protected(w2), protected(w2) / protected(w) - 1)
```

**MEASURED deterministic output:**

```text
selected=[0], protected_before=4.0, protected_after=4.41,
relative_change=0.10250000000000004
```

**DERIVED verdict:** disjoint top sets do not imply orthogonal effects when the protected objective
has cross-coordinate coupling. This is an existence counterexample, not a measurement of either
paper or Pact. It is sufficient to forbid treating contrastive exclusion as a safety proof. Paper B's
MMLU result supplies the real LLM-regime analogue; Pact's joint QP/trust-region/realized replay is the
appropriate receiver-domain certificate.

## Regime caveats and anti-transfer ledger

1. Both methods modify LLM weights. Pact's SegNet and PoseNet are frozen evaluation authority; direct
   transfer is prohibited.
2. `abs(weight) * activation` is not Fisher/margin EV and does not see the inner Jacobian, `R`, uint8,
   collateral flips, pose, or bytes.
3. LLM benchmark percentages, human creativity percentiles, entropy, and embedding homogeneity have
   no conversion to `d_seg`, `d_pose`, or archive bytes.
4. “Forward-only” does not mean zero-cost here. Full n600 scorer forwards, activation capture, storage,
   and batching have their own measured costs and custody requirements.
5. Paper A's one-sample behavior is task/model specific; Paper B's explicit entanglement result shows
   why apparent top-set separation can fail under coupled objectives.
6. Any proposal-tier screen must be evaluated on chronological real states and exact top-set recall;
   synthetic/prompt-only agreement cannot authorize a live controller.
7. Any generator-side parameter mask or delta is video-derived state and must be counted, parsed,
   deterministically replayed, and priced against `25/37_545_489` per byte.
