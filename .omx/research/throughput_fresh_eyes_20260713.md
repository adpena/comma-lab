### 1.2 Async verdict is timely; “free” is not proved

Across the three real logs, every inspected scheduled verdict completed and `verdict_skip_count=0`.
The median worker-service/completion-window ratios are **MEASURED 0.348, 0.303, and 0.343**. This confirms
**no cadence miss**. It does not prove no contention because every run has the worker and none has a
matched same-config no-worker arm. The earlier “47%-duty async-hidden/free” statement is therefore
overstated; the current measured ratios are about 30–34%, and even complete overlap can still slow the
host-side training path through CPU, GIL, memory bandwidth, or thermals.

There is useful but narrower evidence: `kernel_stack_sweep_443_20260711.md` measured an MLX GPU step at
407–412 ms baseline and 409–414 ms under saturating Torch/NumPy CPU co-load, at most about 2% and within
repeat noise. That **MEASURED negative scopes only the GPU step**. It does not identify the whole trainer’s
Python/GIL/host-input penalty. The matched async/solo ticket below is the req-R.

Canonical law:

```text
T_window = max(T_train_concurrent, T_async)
delta_contention = T_train_concurrent / T_train_solo - 1
```

An async-only log identifies cadence misses. Only matched `T_train_solo` and `T_train_concurrent` identify
contention. The executable, fail-closed equation is
`src/tac/canonical_equations/async_overlap_throughput_20260713.py`.
