## Conclusion first

The strong slogan **“implicit beats explicit” is refuted**. The receipt-backed law is conditional:

> Choose the representation with the smallest **complete receiver-closed description and
> realization cost**. Learned implicit carriage is favored for high-dimensional, irregular
> evaluator fields whose shared prior amortizes structure. An optimal explicit grammar is favored
> for stable low-dimensional coordinates with cheap innovations. A hybrid is favored when cheap
> grammar coordinates exist but only a learned or jointly solved realizer survives `R`, uint8, and
> both frozen scorers.

This is not merely cautious wording. Three observed seams force it:

1. PP1's complete explicit partition leg (`173,616 B`) and its learned token-plus-renderer route
   (approximately `177 KB` in that comparison) converge rather than showing strict implicit
   dominance.
2. Static hood support is `139 B` instead of `58,026 B` per-frame support, and exact micro-edits can
   win. These are real counter-instances to “never carry explicitly.”
3. Cheap semantic coordinates do not guarantee cheap legal RGB. The v14 horizon rule realizes only
   `5.2867%` of its cell-space forecast, while DM2 expands `1,569 B` of semantic records into a
   `3,960,549 B` literal RGB construction. Carriage and realization must therefore be selected in
   one action, but they are not the same physical wall.

The inherited “four-instance law” (`pp1`, `sp1`, `lc1`, `ws0`) is not four independent instances.
`pp1`, `sp1`, and `ws0` are correlated variants of high-dimensional explicit semantic carriage;
`lc1` is a direct-label sidecar failure. The honest denominator is **two defect classes**, not four.

\[

The empirical routing prior used to order those complete measurements is

\[

\begin{cases}
\text{OPTIMAL-GRAMMAR}, & d_{\rm eff}\ \text{small and stable, and counted innovation/exceptions are cheap},\\
