## 4. What actually changed, and where the packet got WEAKER

Generation 4 is the first candidate in this packet that is **not a lossless re-encode**.
Every prior generation held the decoded state constant and provably identical, so the whole
improvement was rate and the two distortion terms carried over exactly. That sentence is
false here, and it was load-bearing in a lot of prose.

**Mechanism.** The semantic section is re-quantized under SM3R mode 6 — three FiLM weight
tensors keep only their two highest-L2 rows (bitmask + compact kept-rows block), and a
per-tensor 4-bit depth table drops `frame_embed.weight` and `blocks.0.film.weight` to 3-bit
codes. That damages PoseNet, which reads the frame pair while the semantic renderer produces
only frame 1, so the frame-0 carrier lattice is re-solved at compile time (damped
Gauss-Newton on the receiver-realized Jacobian, then multi-scale integer descent): 6,713 of
7,200 signed-int12 coordinates change for 41 archive bytes, cancelling 99.98% of the leakage
energy in the local solve. The HPAC stream (13,515 B) and the tail (fx2 token stream +
residual + table codes, 109,897 B) are spliced byte-identically from generation 3. The
generation-3 byte-plane serialization split is **off** — the row-prune changes the semantic
body length and the split re-measures negative on the edited body.

**Three places the packet is honestly weaker than generation 3, all recorded rather than
inherited:**

1. **The borrowed-substrate accounting's two largest rows changed class.** Rows 1 and 2 of
   the §2 table classified the semantic renderer state and the pose carrier state as
   `inherited-substrate`, *proven byte-identical to PR #135 after decode*. That byte-identity
   is gone. Both are now `mechanism-adopt-with-attribution`, and §8 says plainly that this is
   not an upgrade in our favour: we took someone else's trained tensors and represented them
   worse on purpose, then repaired the damage to one scorer using their own solver form. The
   edit-then-recompensate pattern is PR #135's and our solver adapts theirs; that is disclosed
   in the accounting, the README, and the PR body.
2. **The CPU axis lost its measurement.** Generation 3's MEASURED_INFEASIBLE adjudication was
   measured on generation 3's bytes and is not transferred. Everywhere the packet used to say
   "measured infeasible on these bytes" it now says "no row exists; the expectation is
   inherited."
3. **Reproduction lost its VERIFIED label.** `experiments/ddm_pq2_compress_e2e.py` has not
   been re-run for these bytes. `PACKET_TARGET.json` records
   `reproduction.status = NOT_RE_VERIFIED_FOR_THESE_BYTES` with the owed item named, and the
   PR body states the scope reduction up front instead of inheriting generation 3's claim.
   What does exist: the compile receipt's fail-closed decoded-state identity assertion before
   build, and receiver parse-back `PASS` at `max_abs_code_deviation = 0`.

**The score arithmetic, since it is no longer pure rate.** Against the intermediate keep01
row (177,576 B, S 0.1571619225142182) the legs are rate −2.6235e-04, seg +1.7400e-04, pose
+2.8407e-05, net −5.994113e-05 — 22.8% of the rate credit retained. I re-derived all three
legs independently from the two receipts rather than copying them, and the exact legs sum to
the net to 1e-18. (The values above are DISPLAYED at 5 significant figures, so adding the
printed strings gives −5.99430e-05 rather than the net; the rounding is in the display, not
in the arithmetic.)

**⚠ CORRECTED 2026-08-19 (round-12 F1, `ddm_rv12f`): the margin was 18.0×; it is 8.97×.**
The net is a DELTA between two independently-8dp-rounded T4 rows, so the two rows' error
bounds **ADD** — 3.336608e-06 (this row) + 3.345782e-06 (keep01) = 6.682391e-06 — and the
margin is 5.994113e-05 / 6.682391e-06 = **8.97×**, not the 17.97× that dividing by one row's
bound alone produces. Dividing a delta by a single row's bound understates the uncertainty by
exactly the other row's contribution, which here is almost exactly 2.00×. **The sign is still
determinate**: 8.97× clears the bar with room. Only the stated margin was wrong, and a cure
whose whole content is "divide by the bound" has to divide by the RIGHT bound. The seg leg
landed near three times its pre-fire model; the ck1 verdict memo
names the cause (a measured CPU→CUDA transfer law exists for pose and none for seg) and the
packet carries that as an open gap rather than smoothing it.

### 4.1 The advisory proves DECODE IDENTITY, not score transfer — and the drift is 19.08× on pose

*(Added 2026-08-19, round-12 F3, `ddm_rv12f`. The packet cited the local advisory run as a
decode-correctness proof on three surfaces without ever printing how far its numbers sit from
the authority's.)*

On the SAME bytes: `d_pose` is **0.00014829** locally against **0.00000777** on the T4 — a
factor of **19.08** — while `d_seg` drifts only **1.43×** (0.00043336 against 0.00030309). The
rate leg is identical by construction. Recomposed, the advisory scores **0.19982** where the
authority row scores **0.15710**; the whole 0.0427 gap is distortion. So the advisory
establishes exactly one thing — that the composed receiver reconstructs the intended decoded
state — and nothing whatever about score transfer. This is now stated on every surface that
cites it: `README_PUBLIC.md`, `REPORT_PUBLIC.txt`, and the spliced `report.txt` block in
`PR_BODY_DRAFT.md`.

The asymmetry is itself the interesting part and is consistent with the vehicle's known
physics: SegNet scores a hard argmax, which absorbs small float perturbations until a pixel
crosses a boundary, while PoseNet regresses six continuous scalars whose MSE has no such
deadband. A cross-microarchitecture render difference therefore shows up ~13× harder on pose
than on seg. That is the same shape as the standing "no seg transfer law exists" gap, seen
from the other side.
