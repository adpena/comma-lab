Four findings, in the order that matters:

1. **Born-small pays an exchange ratio, and it is 97.25×.** `bs2`'s central claim was that
   born-small "is a *different curve*, so it does not pay an exchange ratio at all." Measured in
   the campaign's own currency — damage ÷ rate-credit against the **live best**, break-even 1.0 —
   shipping this container instead of dx2 credits **79,240 B = 0.052763 S** and charges
   **5.131079 S**. **Ratio 97.25×.** It lands on the same ladder as every other rung, **4.50×
   worse than the best measured mechanism (21.62×, tba1-D3)**, worse than tolerance (33.7×) and
   the W72 renderer (35.54×), and between those and ni1 (247.69×). The route does not escape the
   framing `#1245` states; it joins it badly.

   The credit is dx2-relative because the alternative to shipping born-small is shipping **dx2**,
   not shipping HG1-exact — HG1-exact was already refused at +322,422 B. Read *inside* the HG1
   object the same measurement says the opposite thing about the residual, and both readings are
   true of different comparisons; see the closing paragraph of this section.

2. **The charter's pre-registered prediction is FALSIFIED on its pose clause, and that is the more
   valuable half.** Predicted: *"this route dies on SEG, on Lane, and pose is comparatively safe."*
   Measured: **pose carries 75.44% of the damage.** Seg dies too — but **zeroing the entire seg
   damage still leaves pose alone 157.72× over the sub-0.12 bar.** The `tv1`/`tv2` τ-inversion
   (boundary→seg, interior→pose, near-disjoint supports) does not license "boundary-concentrated
   error ⇒ pose-safe" on this object. §5.

3. **Token errors AMPLIFY; they do not absorb — and one class amplifies 228×.** The overall seg
   transfer factor is **τ = 1.113586** against an admission bar of **τ ≤ 0.2114**: **5.27× the
   bar**, and above 1:1. Four classes sit just under 1:1 (τ 0.840–0.983). **Movable sits at
   τ = 228.4** — the generator misses only 1,331 Movable tokens (0.0997% of the miss, its
   *best*-predicted class) and the scorer flips 304,018 Movable pixels (20.45% of the damage), a
   **205× disproportion**. §7 gives the mechanism from source: Movable is coded as **filled
   bounding boxes**, so the generator *manufactures* 499,525 Movable positions rather than losing
   them. **The damage is not where the tokens are wrong; it is where the tokens are wrong about
   something the renderer draws as a region.** A per-position miss table cannot see this, which is
   why neither `hg1` nor `bs2` could have.

4. **Structure HURTS. Placement is now isolated as the mechanism.** `tv1` ran
   `cond_cond` at *exactly* this cardinality (k = 1,334,939) with random-conditional placement:
   ΔS `+3.167471`, τ 0.5295. HG1's *structured* miss at the same k: ΔS `+5.131079`, τ 1.1136 —
   **1.62× more total damage and 2.10× worse τ.** `tv1` §5.3 predicted this comparison would
   isolate placement and that no random probe could show it. It did, and the sign is against the
   route.

**Read inside the HG1 object, the residual was not overpriced — it was a bargain.** Within that
container the 359,280 B residual is exactly what stands between 101,128 B and exactness, and it
buys 5.131079 S of avoided distortion: **1.4282e-05 S/B**, or **21.45× the rate exchange rate**
of 6.658590e-07 S/B. The charter's premise — that the residual is "self-imposed," bought only to
avoid firing a scorer — is right about the *motive* and wrong about the *value*. HG1 paid for the
most valuable bytes in its container and could not know it, because it never fired the scorer.
That is the whole content of this row.

**The byte case is real, re-derived, and irrelevant at this magnitude.** The container does fall
180,368 → 101,128 B (a 79,240 B cut, 36,858 B under the cap). The distortion is 209× the budget
that cut creates. §4.

**And repairing the defect does not save it.** A *perfect* Movable carrier removes 5.0% of the
total damage; repairing **every** seg class removes 24.6% and still leaves the route **157.7×**
over the sub-0.12 bar on pose alone. That bound is worth more than the diagnosis: it says a
successor should not spend a cycle improving this vocabulary class-by-class.

### 2.2 Does the instrument gap threaten the verdict? No — checked in both directions

- **Seg.** CPU→CUDA seg transfer has **no law**; CPU seg deltas are **upper bounds**
  ([[cpu-to-cuda-seg-transfer-has-no-law]]). So `ΔS_seg = +1.260181` may overstate CUDA. Taking
  the adversarial extreme and setting it to **zero**, pose alone is still **157.72×** over the
  sub-0.12 bar.
- **Pose.** `√(10·d_pose)` is concave, so a given `Δd_pose` costs *more* S at a *lower* base. The
  CUDA marginal `5/√(10·d_pose)` is **626.47** vs the local **130.41** — a **4.80×** ratio. For
  small deltas a local pose ΔS **understates** CUDA. Here the candidate's `d_pose = 1.528`
  dominates both bases, so the CUDA ΔS_pose would be `+3.9013` vs my `+3.8709` — **larger by
  0.030**. The instrument gap moves the answer in the *unfavourable* direction and by a
  negligible amount.

**Neither correction can rescue the route.** That is the only claim I make about transfer.

### 2.3 Consumption proved twice, by digest

"The generator field scored the same as base" and "the substitution was silently ignored" predict
identical output. Both are excluded by measurement, not assumption:

1. **The receiver's own decode receipt.** `f26_inflate` wrote
   `.f26_decode_checkpoints/tokens_cpu_stage_complete.json` recording
   `decoded_token_sha256 = 2884c570…` and `cache_reuse.status = HIT`. The base row's receipt at
   the same path records `cc10a7b0…`. **Two rows, two different fields, each self-reported by the
   consuming receiver.** The cache key `6e630e9f…` was computed by the receiver's own
   `_token_cache_binding` from the archive it decodes — never hand-typed — and is identical in
   both rows, which is what makes them comparable.
2. **The rendered frames differ.** Candidate `0.raw` sha
   `43c359eadd7c6e263adf7a1e2732a2b34948b1db8681bcc1be8f7c493b2ac841`; base `0.raw` sha
   `7246a4ff8f79b03ab14b3a72f6a6e2fff18b567fcb61f12a7fe311d48f5f2de7`. Same 3,662,409,600 bytes,
   different content.

**What this control CANNOT rule out** — stated because a control whose limits are unstated is a
liability:

- It does **not** prove the *whole* field was consumed. It proves the receiver loaded a blob with
  that digest and that the frames changed. A receiver that read only part of the field would
  produce both signals. Nothing here bounds partial consumption; the field-level positive control
  that would (`tv1`'s `k = 0` byte-identity) was run by `tv1`, not re-run by me.
- It says nothing about **pose**. The seg positive control in §3 re-derives the seg axis only;
  `d_pose` is read from the row's own report and is not independently reproduced by this arm.
- It cannot distinguish **"the receiver is correct" from "the receiver is consistently wrong"** —
  both rows share one receiver, which is the point (matched instrument) and also the limit.

### 3.3 Which of the two coding questions the miss loads

`hc1` measured that dx2's token stream is **97.80% one binary question** — the indicator *"is my
argmax right?"* costs **111,275.62 B** at 2.6917 bits, while the 4-way *"which class instead"*
costs only **2,500.54 B** at 0.0879 bits. HG1's misses load **the expensive question, harder**:
they are 1,334,939 *wrong-here* events against dx2's HPAC model's total uncertainty mass of
**339,034 positions** (`tv1` §1). The analytic generator is **3.94× worse as a predictor** than
the learned HPAC model it replaces, while its vocabulary costs **3.53×** more than the model
(47,667 vs 13,515 B). It is worse on both axes at once, which is why its residual is large.

The *which-class* half is almost free for HG1 too, and its concentration is extreme: Lane→Road is
97.0% of Lane's miss, MyCar→Road is 99.6% of MyCar's, Undrivable→Movable is 87.3% of
Undrivable's. Whatever a successor pays, it will pay it to say **where**, not **which** —
[[perfect-localization-is-worthless-the-address-is-the-tax]] again, from inside this field.

**Four classes transfer at just under 1:1** (τ 0.840–0.983). That is already the refutation of the

**The fifth class is the finding. Movable's τ is 228.4.** The generator misses only **1,331**

**The transferable law, which is new here:** *the damage is not where the tokens are wrong; it is
where the tokens are wrong about something the renderer draws as a region.* A per-position miss
table cannot see this, and neither `hg1` nor `bs2` could have — both stopped at the token field.

## 4. The byte case, re-derived from dx2's actual composition

`ar1b`'s census is exact and I reproduced its closure: `31 + 14 + 13,515 + 30,856 + 22,010 + 96 +
47 + 22 = 66,591`, `66,591 + 113,777 = 180,368`, **remainder 0**. From components its S recomputes
to `0.14821987563243377` — all 17 digits of the pointer.

| | dx2 | HG1 residual-dropped | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| framing (ZIP hdr + name + CD + EOCD, or HG1's own) | 114 | 499 | **+385** |
| HPAC probability model | 13,515 | 0 | −13,515 |
| semantic renderer | 30,856 | 30,856 | 0 |

| **total** | **180,368** | **101,128** | **−79,240** |

**The clarification the charter asked for.** `bs2`'s "79,625 B saved" is a **leg** figure
(127,292 − 47,667) and is correct as one. The **container** falls by **79,240 B**, because HG1
spends **385 B more on framing** than dx2 does. `bs2`'s container arithmetic — 101,128 B and
36,858 B of margin — reproduces **exactly**, so the charter's suspicion that the two bodies were
confused did not find an error; it found a 385 B framing difference that changes no conclusion.
I record it because the container number is what prices the route and the leg number is not.

Also verified rather than inherited: the sub-0.12 cap is a **strict-inequality floor** —
137,986 B gives S = 0.1199994415 (passes) and 137,987 B gives 0.1200001074 (fails), both at dx2's
distortion. The zero-distortion cap is 180,218 B; 180,368 − 180,218 = **150 B**, and
180,368 − 137,986 = **42,382 B**. The charter's two readings of the demand both check.

**The two bars this creates, and they are alternatives, not a joint bound:**

| bar | meaning | budget |
|---|---|---:|
| **A** | merely beat dx2's S = 0.14821987563243377 | `ΔS_dist ≤ 0.052762667` |
| **B** | reach sub-0.12 at the 101,128 B container | `ΔS_dist ≤ 0.024542788` |

The charter's `d_seg ≤ 4.4682e-04` and `d_pose ≤ 1.0578e-04` are the two **corners** of bar B —
each assumes the *other* axis is held exactly at dx2's value. They are not simultaneously
satisfiable requirements and neither is a standalone bound. Measured, both are exceeded by 29.0×
and 14,443× respectively, so the distinction does not bite here; I state it because a successor
quoting one corner without the other would be quoting half a budget.

## 5. The pre-registered prediction, adjudicated

> **Predicted (charter):** *"this route dies on SEG, on Lane, and pose is comparatively safe."*
> **Falsifier (charter):** *"if pose is what fails, the τ-inversion does not transfer to this
> object and that is a more valuable finding than the verdict."*

**The falsifier FIRED.**

| | ΔS | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| seg | +1.260181 | 24.56% |
| **pose** | **+3.870898** | **75.44%** |
| total | +5.131079 | |

Pose damage is **3.07×** seg damage. The route dies on **both**, and **pose first**.

**The "on Lane" clause is also only half right.** Lane is savaged — 46.61% of every Lane pixel
flips — but it contributes **21.1%** of the seg damage, behind **Road at 36.1%** and level with
**Movable at 20.5%**. Lane is 0.586% of the field, so it *cannot* dominate a mean taken over the
whole grid no matter how completely it fails. The campaign's Lane-first instinct is right about
*fragility* and wrong about *mass*, and this row separates the two for the first time on this
object.

**Why the τ-inversion did not transfer.** `tv1`/`tv2` measured boundary positions driving seg
(4.33× / 3.44× position effect) and interior positions driving pose (2.73× / 2.38×), on
**near-disjoint supports**. The charter combined that with HG1's 23.38× boundary enrichment to
predict a seg-loaded, pose-light failure. Three reasons the inference does not hold, and only the
third is a defect in the reasoning rather than in the object:

1. **The τ-inversion is a statement about *marginal* perturbations of a few positions.** It was
   measured at k ≤ 10⁵ single-token reassignments. HG1's miss is not a perturbation — it deletes
   44.77% of a class and manufactures 34.11% of another. At that amplitude the linearization the
   inversion lives in does not apply.
2. **Boundary-concentration is not interior-emptiness.** 49.35% of HG1's miss is *off* the
   boundary — 676,210 positions on it, 658,729 off it. The "interior" channel that `tv1`/`tv2`
   found drives pose is loaded with two thirds of a million positions, which is exactly the pose
   actuator those arms identified.
3. **The prediction quietly required τ_pose < τ_seg, and nothing measured it.** The inversion
   ranks *which axis a position type loads*; it never claimed the pose channel saturates more
   slowly. Reading a *relative* ranking as an *absolute* safety margin is the same class of error
   as [[the-instruments-own-units-level-and-aggregation-are-part-of-the-claim-20260816]]. That is
   my correction to the charter's reasoning, and it generalizes: **the τ-inversion routes damage,
   it does not bound it.**

### 5.1 What this routes, and what it does not

**Do not spend a cycle improving this vocabulary class-by-class.** The §3.4 counterfactual is the
reason: the single most disproportionate cell in the whole measurement is worth 5.0% of the
damage, and every seg class together is worth 24.6%.

**Two things this row makes cheaper for whoever goes next.**

1. **Pose is now the measured wall on this route, and nothing here explains it.** 75.44% of the
   damage, no per-class decomposition possible, and `d_pose` reached **1.528** — 10,395× the base.
   `bs2` §2.4 already argued the live cheap instrument is `tri1` rank 2 / E2′ (put pose in the
   objective, retrain, re-measure `ap1 carrier_l1`). This row raises its value: it is the only
   queued measurement aimed at the term that actually decides this family.
2. **The tolerance question is answered for structured placement, and the answer is the opposite
   of the hopeful one.** `tv1` supplies the random-placement curve; this row supplies the
   structured point at the same cardinality; the difference (1.62× total, 2.10× on τ) isolates
   placement. A successor proposing *any* lossy field representation now has a measured prior that
   **structure hurts** — its errors will be where the renderer draws regions, and that is exactly
   where the scorer looks.

## 6. Which of the three 08-23 closures actually reopen — and one does not

`sy2`'s object-change law says a closed leg survives only if another leg first **changes the
object it was priced on**. The charter asked me to name that change for each closure in one
sentence, or to say plainly that the closure still binds. Adversarially, one of the three still
binds.

| closure | container | what the residual-drop changes | does the closure still bind? |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **HG1** | 460,408 B | Deletes the 359,280 B unique-home residual — **78.04% of its container** — moving it from +322,422 B over the cap to **36,858 B under** it, i.e. its refusal was caused *entirely* by the section being dropped. | **NO** — and HG1 says so itself: its first LIVE-HYPOTHESIS is *"a genuine object change can make a previously closed representation leg useful … HG1 only re-encodes the current categorical field."* |
| **HR3** | 463,601 B | Deletes its 362,473 B residual-equivalent, which **includes the 3,223 B learned INR** whose only job was to shrink that residual; 463,601 − 362,473 = **101,128 B**, *numerically identical* to HG1's dropped container, because what remains is the same inherited 52,962 + generators 47,667 + framing 499. | **NO** — it collapses onto HG1's object exactly. But HR3 contributes a constraint the drop does **not** remove: its INR removed only **104 B (0.03%)** of the residual, so no "shrink it instead of dropping it" middle path exists. |
| **ET1** | 535,761 B | **NOTHING.** ET1 has no residual section to drop. Its 482,678 B *is* the implicit space-time BSP topology, which reconstructs the field exactly by construction. Setting "the residual" to zero is undefined for ET1; its container stays 535,761 B, **+397,775 B over the cap**. | **YES — ET1 STILL BINDS.** |

**This corrects the charter's framing.** The charter presented the residual-drop as reopening

refuses the one that reopened, on distortion, at 209× the budget.

## 7. Causal legality of the generators — checked at source, and it is clean

The sibling arm `hc1` established that boundary-distance statistics in wide circulation are
**acausal**: computed from the frame being coded, which the receiver has not yet decoded. Valid as
description, illegal as design input. It asked whether HG1's generators carry the same defect at
the level of the route — because they run at **decode** time, and a generator that conditions on
the field it is generating is not realizable at any score.

**They are causally legal. The fit/render split is clean, and I verified it by reading the
source, not the names.**

- Encode side may read the truth, and does: `fit_horizon_payload(tokens)`,
  `fit_lane_payload(tokens)`, `fit_movable_payload(tokens)`, `fit_mycar_payload(tokens)`.
- Decode side reads **only transmitted bytes**:
  `render_generators(streams, output_path)` allocates a fresh memmap and calls
  `render_horizon(payload, output)`, `render_lane(payload, output)`,
  `render_movable(payload, output)`, `render_mycar(payload, output)`. **Not one render function
  takes the target field**, and none is passed it.

Two honest riders. (a) The four renders composite in a fixed order into one buffer, so a later
render overwrites an earlier one's output — that is decoder-internal state the decoder legitimately
holds, not target access. (b) `component_boxes(frame)` *does* read `frame == CLASS_MOVABLE`, but
it is called only from `fit_movable_payload`, on the encode side.

**And the source read pays for itself, because it hands over the mechanism of §3.2.**
`render_movable` is literally `output[pair, y0:y1, x0:x1] = CLASS_MOVABLE` over transmitted
`(pair, track, y0, x0, y1, x1)` rows. Movable is a **bounding box**, filled solid. That is why the
generated field carries **34.11% more Movable** than the truth, and why Road→Movable (264,776) and
Undrivable→Movable (229,282) are the dominant confusions of the two largest classes. The
hallucination is not a fitting failure — it is the parameterization, and no amount of fitting fixes
a box that must cover a car.

I did **not** consume `df1`'s `flip_flags.npy` anywhere in this arm, so the `bitorder='little'`
hazard `hc1` documented has no surface here. The 23.38× boundary-enrichment and 40.81× Lane
over-representation I use are properties of the **generator's output versus the truth** —
descriptive, encode-side, and not a decoder-side conditioning — which is the use `hc1` explicitly
licensed.

## 9. Corrections to the charter

Four, in descending value.

1. **The pose prediction was wrong, and the charter invited the correction.** §5. Pose carries
   75.44%, not "comparatively safe." The transferable form: **the τ-inversion routes damage
   between axes; it does not bound damage on either.** A relative ranking read as an absolute
   safety margin is [[the-instruments-own-units-level-and-aggregation-are-part-of-the-claim-20260816]].

2. **"Those three closures" is two.** §6. ET1 has no residual section; the residual-drop changes
   nothing about its object and its refusal at +397,775 B stands untouched. The charter's
   instruction to state the object-change per closure *or say the closure still binds* is what
   surfaced this — the instruction worked exactly as designed.

3. **The byte-margin suspicion did not find an error.** §4. The charter suspected `bs2` had
   conflated "generators replace 127,292 B" with dx2's 113,777 B token stream. It had not: 127,292
   is tokens **+ HPAC model**, both of which the generators genuinely replace. The real refinement
   is smaller and different — the **container** saving is 79,240 B, not the 79,625 B **leg**
   saving, because HG1 spends 385 B more on framing. `bs2`'s 101,128 B and 36,858 B are exact.

4. **`bs2`'s central qualitative claim is now measured and false.** §0. "Born-small is a different
   curve, so it does not pay an exchange ratio at all" — it pays **97.25×**, which places it on the
   ladder between W72 (35.54×) and ni1 (247.69×) and **4.50× worse than the best measured rung**.
   The framing `#1245` states is not escaped by changing basis; it is the currency every basis is
   priced in.

**A method finding worth carrying: my own positive control fired, and its UNIT was the bug.**
The first per-class pass refused with a delta of 8.719e-09 against a tolerance of 6e-09. That
looked like a cross-instrument failure. It was not. `d_seg` is a mean of 600 rationals with
denominator 196,608, so its physical quantum is **one argmax pixel = 8.478e-9** — *larger* than
the 5e-9 half-width of the report's own 8-dp rounding. **A tolerance stated in float ulps below
one pixel cannot be satisfied except by luck**, and mine was. The real disagreement was
**exactly one pixel in 117,964,800**, caused by my `--batch-size 8` against `evaluate.py`'s
default of 16: CPU BLAS blocks differently, and a near-tie logit pair argmaxes the other way.
Re-run at batch 16, both controls pass at **0.000 pixels beyond rounding**.

I record this rather than quietly widening the number because the failure mode is
[[the-instruments-own-units-level-and-aggregation-are-part-of-the-claim-20260816]] applied to a
*control*: I had written a gate whose threshold was finer than the thing it was gating could
resolve. The fix was to state the band in pixels and report the realized pixel delta, not to
loosen a float. The same pass also found — and this is the more serious of the two — that the
control **raised before the payload was written**, so its first refusal discarded twenty minutes
of computed argmax fields. That is the measure-and-discard class in its purest form, inside a
script whose whole job is retention. Payload writes now precede the verdict, the refusal is
recorded in the retained JSON, and the process still exits non-zero.

**One correction I owe about my own method.** The strongest control available to this row — a
`k = 0` republication of dx2's own field through *my* invocation, checked for `0.raw` byte-identity
with `7246a4ff…` — I did **not** run. I relied on `tv1`'s `k0_control`, which used the same
runtime tree, the same GT lineage, and the same `fire_local_advisory` path, and which is byte-
verified against the matched base. That reliance is sound but it is an **inherited** control, not
**my** control, and the honest statement of my instrument is: *identical tree, identical path,
control run by a sister arm.* A second row would have cost ~14 min and removed the word
"inherited." I judged the 209× margin made it non-decisive; a closer row would have obliged me to
run it.
