## A. Faithful paper summary (confirmed-fetched)

**Title (verbatim):** "Anatomy of Post-Training: Using Interpretability to Characterize Data and Shape the Learning Signal"
**arXiv:** 2606.12360v1 (posted ~2026-06; v1).
**Authors (17):** Leon Bergen, Usha Bhalla, Sidharth Baskaran, Max Loeffler, Raphael Sarfati, Dhruvil Gala, Ryan Panwar, Santiago Aranguri, Thomas Fel, Atticus Geiger, Matthew Kowal, Siddharth Boppana, Daniel Balsam, Owen Lewis, Jack Merullo, Thomas McGrath, Ekdeep Singh Lubana. (Anthropic-affiliated per PDF metadata.)

**Abstract (verbatim):**
> "Language-model post-training is the main stage at which model behavior is shaped, yet it still largely involves optimization of scalar rewards that summarize diverse desiderata. This abstraction gives practitioners little visibility into what their data actually teaches models, allowing spurious correlations to be learned by a model and inducing undesirable behaviors such as over-stylization and sycophancy. To address this problem, we ask: can we inspect a preference dataset before optimization and decide, at the level of concepts, which behaviors a model should be allowed to learn? Motivated by this, we introduce a data-centric post-training pipeline that uses interpretability protocols to develop statistical hypotheses for the latent concepts separating preferred from dispreferred generations, making them explicit for fine-grained user feedback. Building on this view, we unify several interpretability-based training protocols as ways of shaping rewards via feature or data interventions. Empirically, we show that our pipeline diagnoses undesirable signals in existing preference data, mitigates off-target learning, and can also help amplify or shape desired properties such as safeguards and model personality. More broadly, our results suggest that interpretability can turn post-training from optimizing opaque proxy rewards into a process of auditing and sculpting the learning signal itself."

**Core contribution.** A *data-centric* LLM-post-training pipeline that, before DPO optimization, uses interpretability (SAEs) to form statistical hypotheses about which *latent concepts* separate preferred vs dispreferred generations, surfaces them for human feedback, and then *intervenes* on the reward/data so the model learns only the intended concepts (mitigating spurious correlation, over-stylization, sycophancy).

**Method (faithful, from the arXiv-HTML render):**
- **Interpretability protocol = Sparse Autoencoders.** BatchTopK SAEs trained on Layer-24 representations; SAE features clustered by activation statistics to find behavior-corresponding subspaces; auto-interpreted by an LLM to name clusters. Compared against linear probes, attention probes, difference-of-means steering vectors, ReFT modules.
- **Hypothesis-generation pipeline:** (1) run SAE over prompt+response tokens, max-pool features; (2) cluster into prompt-feature clusters `A_k` and response-delta clusters `R_m`; (3) prompt-conditioned effect `Δ_{k,m} = E[u_{i,m} | i∈S(k)] − E[u_{i,m} | i∉S(k)]`; (4) feature-conditioned selectivity via Welch two-sample tests; (5) rank by effect size, validate with split-half; (6) interactive viewer for human inspection.
- **Four reward-shaping interventions** (unified as "explaining away" an undesired concept):
  1. **Reward shaping:** `r'(x,y) = r(x,y) − λ·s_u(x,y)` (subtract a concept score from the reward).
  2. **Activation steering:** push representations along the concept direction so the reward no longer incentivizes learning it.
  3. **Inoculation prompting:** condition on a prompt that explicitly asks for the concept, so the model attributes it to the temporary context.
  4. **Data filtering:** remove/downweight training units whose preference signal is well-explained by the concept.
- Interventions applied during **DPO** optimization; behavioral change measured on benchmarks.

**Experimental setup.** Models: Llama-3.1-8B/70B, OLMo-3-7B, OLMo-3.1-32B. Data: Dolci (preference), LMSys + FineWeb (SAE training), OLMES (capability), HarmBench + XSTest (safety), Alpaca (eval).

**Headline results.** Feature-conditioned hypothesis pipeline: **R²=0.9** predicted-vs-actual feature change post-DPO (prompt-conditioned R²=0.58, lower because prompt clusters are rare ~0.1%). Reward shaping most consistently raises harmful-refusal scores while retaining DPO accuracy gains; synthetically-poisoned traits (e.g. "Goblin Weave", cheerfulness) mitigated across interventions (token filtering underperforms for diffuse traits, better for localized ones).

**Stated limitations.** (a) Assumes target behaviors are statistically independent (Eq. 3); concept correlations cause off-target effects ("many interventions induce strong off-target effects"). (b) SAE feature-splitting caps performance. (c) Prompt-conditioned view unreliable for rare prompt clusters. (d) Inoculation prompting gave inconsistent/no safeguard improvement. (e) Activation-steering can distort the very direction being amplified.

### B.1 The terminological false-friend (caught, not fallen for)
The phrase "shape rewards via feature or data interventions" superficially rhymes with our score-domain Lagrangian work (Lever 2 of the in-curriculum memo) and with "sensitivity-map" / "bit-allocator" hooks. **This is a false friend.** Their "reward" is an LLM preference scalar over text generations; ours is the contest score over rendered video frames. Their "feature intervention" is an SAE-direction edit on a transformer's residual stream; ours is a per-tensor weight re-quant or a per-pixel margin weight. There is no equation, dataset, or gradient path in common. Per the NO-FAKE rule (forbidden: borrowed-substrate-passed-as-original / surrogate trap), I explicitly refuse to manufacture a relevance bridge from the vocabulary overlap.

### B.2 Could SAE-style interpretability help us *audit our own scorers*?
One could imagine training an SAE on SegNet/PoseNet activations to "find concepts" the scorer keys on, to guide where the decoder should spend bytes. **This is a leap the paper does not make and does not support** — it works on LLM residual streams, not CNN/ViT vision features, and its whole pipeline (preference clusters, DPO interventions, auto-interp by an LLM) does not transfer. We *already* have a better, more direct, measured tool for "where does the scorer key on": the **SegNet logit-margin map** (`tac.substrates.d1_segnet_margin_polytope.margin_map.compute_logit_margin_map`, `tac.analysis.segnet_boundary_marginals`) and **per-tensor score-sensitivity gradients** (`||∂S/∂w_t||`, the basis of Lever 4 score-aware QAT). Those are exact, differentiable, and already wired. An SAE-on-the-scorer would be a strictly worse, indirect re-derivation of signal we already measure. NOT worth pursuing.

### B.3 The single honest oblique transfer (research-prior, NOT a score lever)
The paper's *meta-thesis* — "don't optimize an opaque scalar reward; audit and sculpt the learning signal at the concept level so you don't learn spurious correlations" — is, abstractly, the **same instinct** behind our standing discipline that the score-domain Lagrangian must train against the *actual* d_seg/d_pose terms (HNeRV parity L6) rather than an L2/reconstruction proxy that "learns" pixels the scorer's argmax ignores. In our framing: an L2 reconstruction loss IS the "opaque proxy reward that learns spurious correlations" (fidelity the scorer never reads), and the boundary-concentrated d_seg surrogate + score-aware QAT ARE the "shaping the learning signal at the concept level." **This is a confirmation of a discipline we already hold, not a new lever.** It supplies zero new code, zero new equation, and moves zero terms of S. Logged here only so the signal is not lost: the paper is a (cross-domain) external corroboration that proxy-reward optimization breeds off-target learning — which is exactly why our levers are score-domain, not reconstruction-domain. No action follows.
