## Online cross-check: useful priors, not transferred evidence

The literature search was restricted to primary paper/project pages and changes only the design
prior for a new generator:

- [NerVast (WACV 2026)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/html/Lee_NerVast_Compression-Efficient_Scaling_of_Implicit_Neural_Video_Representations_via_Scene-based_WACV_2026_paper.html)
  reports scene-based parameter sharing across implicit video representations. This makes shared
  scene structure a plausible way to buy capacity without linear per-frame packet growth.
- [T-NeRV (CVPR 2024)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Saethre_Combining_Frame_and_GOP_Embeddings_for_Neural_Video_Representation_CVPR_2024_paper.html)
  combines frame and GOP embeddings with entropy-constrained training. The relevant inference is a
  coarse-to-fine time code, not a transferred byte or distortion number.
- [DS-NeRV (CVPR 2024)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Yan_DS-NeRV_Implicit_Neural_Video_Representation_with_Decomposed_Static_and_Dynamic_CVPR_2024_paper.html)
  separates static and dynamic codes at different sampling rates. That matches GF1's error
  decomposition better than uniformly widening all four streams.
- [C3 (CVPR 2024 project)](https://c3-neural-compression.github.io/) jointly optimizes synthesis,
  latents, and entropy modeling per object. It supports measuring a richer generator under its real
  coded packet, not parameter count. None of these papers proves a Pact row.

RNeRV's architecture/training ablations were also checked at its
[WACV 2026 primary paper page](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/html/Gwilliam_How_to_Design_and_Train_Your_Implicit_Neural_Representation_for_WACV_2026_paper.html).
The transferable lesson is only that capacity placement matters; the contest's task-space target and
rule-118 accounting remain different objects.
