The charter asked for eight axes. Eight is the count of *score readouts*; it is not the count of

Three consequences, each of which decides rows in §4:

1. **`seg` has exactly two actuators: tokens and renderer.** Any pair `X × seg` with
   X ∉ {tokens, renderer} has no seg handle inside the pair — it is a **disguised triple**. This
   kills `carrier × seg` and `hpac × seg` as pairs.
   *Search scope for this negative-existence claim: the six rate blocks of the ar1b census (zero
   remainder), with ap1's per-class SegNet response for carrier and residual, ms9/mst1's stage
   attribution for tokens vs renderer, and w72's bit-identical-token receipt. I did not search
   outside the dx2 census.*
2. **Where the seg or pose handle IS the other leg, the "pair" is one actuator read twice.**
   `renderer × seg`, `renderer × pose` and `tokens × seg` all demand that a single object become
   simultaneously smaller and better. That is not composition; it is fb1's single-axis case with
   two labels, and it is exactly where the measured tension sits.
3. **HPAC's only non-destructive move is a better model of a *changed* field.** On the current
   field the sharp-optimum law says no better model exists; coarsening it destroys exact token
   reconstruction. So HPAC has one legal escape and it requires the token leg. That is
   `tokens × hpac`, and it is why that pair — not any other — is the one that survives.

This does not re-privilege "jointness." `ddm_gv1` **REFUTED** that (joint 8/49 = 16.33% vs
single-axis 14/65 = 21.54%, Fisher OR 0.7108, p = 0.6326; campaign phase carries the signal, not
